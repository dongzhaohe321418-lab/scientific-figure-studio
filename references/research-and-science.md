# Research and scientific validation

## Build a specification, not a list of attractive materials

1. Identify the actual object, operating mode and architecture. A reference image may be stylised, incomplete or incorrect. Label identification confidence; do not assign an exact make or model from appearance alone.
2. Prefer primary articles and supplementary methods for devices; manufacturer manuals and optical/mechanical diagrams for instruments. Use reviews to orient the search, then check the claim at its source. Open relevant full figures and captions, not only search thumbnails or abstracts.
3. Separate **structural evidence** from **visual inspiration**. A reference with appealing shading need not describe the chosen device architecture. Resolve conflicting source variants explicitly rather than combining incompatible layers.
4. Make a component table: ID, name/material, location, neighbours/interfaces, function, relevant dimensions, evidence, and simplification. Use unknown values rather than invented thicknesses, wavelengths, band offsets, resolutions or efficiencies.
5. Make an arrow table: origin, destination, species/signal, direction, condition and legend. Check physical continuity across panels. Define whether arrows mean particle motion, conventional current, light propagation, heat or a procedural step.
6. Check nomenclature, formulae, subscripts, superscripts, charge signs, units, conditions and panel references. Derive quantitative plots or geometries from supplied data/equations with deterministic tools. Image generation must not fabricate measurements, microscopy, spectra or simulation results.

## Domain checks to adapt, not universal templates

- Semiconductor devices: terminal connectivity, junction polarity, carrier selectivity, active region, blocking/passivation layers and optical access. Electron motion and conventional current point in opposite directions under the same local transport condition; an unspecified decorative arrow cannot stand for both.
- Tandem photovoltaics: specify two-terminal versus four-terminal architecture and the particular top/bottom subcells. In a series two-terminal device, distinguish equal operating current from a claim that the isolated photocurrents are automatically matched. Do not mix contact schemes from incompatible papers.
- QLEDs: distinguish electrical carrier injection/transport and recombination from optical pumping or lasing. HTL/ETL functions are not represented merely by relabelling coloured slabs. Check carrier origins, transport paths and the emission region against the selected architecture.
- qPCR instruments: identify sample vessels, thermal block/heater arrangement, excitation, spectral selection, collection and detector. Scanning and imaging implementations differ. Do not depict one optical layout as universal or assign a manufacturer's proprietary design without evidence.
- Exploded apparatus: preserve assembly relationships and the meaning of fluid, vacuum, optical and thermal connections. Exploded spacing is a layout device, not an operational gap. Avoid attractive floating parts whose connections are physically impossible.
- Biological/chemical mechanisms: verify stoichiometry, molecular identity, compartment boundaries and directionality. Schematic molecules are not validated structures. Use authoritative structure data when molecular geometry matters.

## Sources record

For each important claim, record a source URL/DOI, title, relevant figure/section, claim supported, date accessed and uncertainty. For visual references, record the actual figure inspected and the specific transferable design features. Do not reproduce a source's distinctive illustration or upload its image to a generative service unless reuse is permitted and within task authorisation. When it is not, describe general visual features in an original composition.

If browsing is unavailable, use provided sources or stable established knowledge only within its limits. Mark source-dependent structure as provisional; never pretend that research occurred. For “latest”, niche or uncertain claims, obtain current authoritative evidence before finalising them.

## Scientific caption

State the selected architecture and message, explain colour/arrow conventions, and identify grouped layers, schematic particle sizes and non-scale thicknesses. Distinguish a conceptual illustration from measured data. A note saying “not to scale” does not excuse wrong topology or misleading geometry.
